# UNESCO Youth Hackathon 2026 — Participant Brief

**Theme:** "Play Your Part: Youth Designing the Future of Media and Information Literacy (MIL)"
**Organizer:** UNESCO Media and Information Literacy (MIL) Unit
**Event window:** 15 June 2026 – 14 October 2026 (winners ceremony extends to Nov)
**Official page:** https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-youth-hackathon-2026
**Contact:** MILunit@unesco.org

---

## 1. What It Is

A flagship initiative of UNESCO's Global Media and Information Literacy (MIL) Week. It invites youth worldwide to design practical solutions — not necessarily software — that strengthen media and information literacy: helping people critically navigate AI-generated content, misinformation, disinformation, and today's information ecosystem.

The 2025 edition drew **1,200+ teams from 138 countries**, so expect a large, competitive, globally distributed applicant pool.

---

## 2. Eligibility & Team Rules

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | All team members must be 18–30 |
| Team size | 2–6 members |
| Team composition | National, regional, or international teams allowed |
| Experience | None required — open to students, developers, educators, activists, entrepreneurs |
| Values alignment | Teams should align with UNESCO values (peace, diversity, freedom of expression) |
| Other | Gender balance and inclusion encouraged (not mandatory) |

---

## 3. Challenge Tracks (choose one or more)

1. **AI and MIL** — solutions addressing challenges posed by AI through an MIL lens (e.g., deepfake detection, AI content provenance, critical-AI-use education)
2. **MIL Education** — creative approaches to teaching MIL in the digital age
3. **Community Impact** — MIL-based interventions that empower communities
4. **Youth Engagement** — strategies positioning youth organizations as MIL change agents
5. **Open Track** — any other MIL-related idea fitting the theme

*Given an agentic-systems/AI background, the **AI and MIL** track is the most natural fit.*

---

## 4. Accepted Proposal Formats

The competition is explicitly **not limited to tech builds**. Accepted formats include:

- Games
- Applications/Websites
- Radio programmes/Podcasts
- Artistic work (comics, short videos, documentaries, etc.)
- Educational toolkits
- Youth organization campaigns
- Community-based interventions
- Other creative modalities

**Important:** You do **not** need to build the actual working product for submission — a well-developed concept/prototype is sufficient.

---

## 5. Submission Requirements

Each team submits **two components**, in English:

### A. Proposal Document (PDF or Word)
Must include:
- Team members
- Problem statement
- Objectives
- Target audience
- Prototype or concept
- Sustainability
- Creativity
- Feasibility

Keep it clear, concise, well-structured, and readable.

### B. Pitch Video (max. 3 minutes)
Should cover:
- **The story** — what problem you're solving and why it matters
- **The solution** — what the project is, how it works, who benefits
- **Originality & impact** — what makes it unique and what impact it could have

---

## 6. Judging Focus

UNESCO states the evaluation prioritizes **impact, creativity, feasibility, and relevance** over technical sophistication alone — this is not a pure coding competition. Submissions are reviewed by an international jury. (UNESCO has not published a detailed weighted rubric for the 2026 edition publicly; the criteria embedded in the proposal document requirements — problem statement, objectives, target audience, prototype/concept, sustainability, creativity, feasibility — function as the de facto evaluation lens.)

## 8. Mentoring Programme

- Free, online, open to **all registered participants** (not just finalists)
- Scheduled **1–2 July 2026**
- Features experts in MIL, digital transformation, AI, project development, and innovation
- **Live slots are limited (first-come, first-served)** — register and reserve early
- Those who miss live sessions still get recordings and resource materials

---

## 9. Prizes / What Winners Get

- No cash prize is publicly stated.
- **Winning teams are invited by UNESCO to the Voices European Festival of Journalism and Media Freedom**, Thessaloniki, Greece, 26–28 November 2026, to showcase their project to an international audience of stakeholders.
- Historically (per UNESCO's general Youth Hackathon page), UNESCO has covered **flights and accommodation** for winning representatives to attend the culminating event — confirm this detail for the 2026 edition specifically once official rules/FAQ are released, as terms can vary by year.
- All teams that successfully submit receive a **Participation Certificate** from UNESCO, regardless of outcome.
- Typically **around 4 winning teams** are selected, though this can vary by edition.
